### SLIDE 5 — The Solution (60 seconds)

**Three pillars:**

**1. 🎯 Smart Buddy Matching — powered by Amazon Bedrock**
- New relocator fills a 2-min form (family, origin, hobbies, needs)
- AI matches them against all settled TasNetworks employees
- The first message they get in Hobart is from a real colleague — not a portal
- **Guaranteed before Day 1** — not after, not "if the leader remembers"

**2. 🗺️ The 90-Day Life Map**
- Beyond the checklist — GP, licence, schools, community, discounts
- Surfaces real TasNetworks benefits most employees don't know exist:
  - $250 wellbeing rebate, AccessEAP 1800 818 728, free flu vax, onsite gym
- Crowdsourced — gets smarter every time someone settles well

**3. 💚 Fortnightly Pulse Check — Amazon Bedrock AI**
- 2 questions, every 14 days, triggered by EventBridge
- **Catches the 3–6 month drift window** — exactly when 29% of leavers are quietly deciding
- Sentiment analysis flags at-risk employees before disengagement → exit
- HR only gets notified when someone needs it — care, not surveillance

## 💬 JUDGE Q&A — READY ANSWERS

**"How is this different from the buddy program that already exists on the checklist?"**
> "Three things. First, it's only assigned 71% of the time — TasNetworks' own Week 1 survey. One in three new starters doesn't get one. Anchor guarantees 100%, before Day 1. Second, the existing buddy is ad hoc and work-focused. Anchor matches on life context — same origin city, family situation, real needs. Third, the existing buddy has no structure. Anchor runs a 90-day framework with fortnightly check-ins to make sure the connection sticks."

**"What does the survey data actually prove?"**
> "TasNetworks' own CultureAmp data shows the engagement cliff: 97% feel welcome at Week 1. By Month 3, only 68% are confident in their systems and only 53% understand the A+D process. In the exit survey, 29% had been considering leaving for 3–6 months before they went — that's the window Anchor's pulse operates in. And in the Month 3 open-text responses, new starters are literally asking for buddies and mentors. We didn't invent this need. TasNetworks employees described it."

**"What about privacy — can you actually use employee data for matching?"**
> "Yes, with consent — and we've read TasNetworks' Privacy Policy, dated August 2025. The matching is opt-in, consent-gated, and built to Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and the TAS Personal Information Protection Act 2004. Data minimisation principle applies — we only collect what's needed for matching. This is actually a design feature: it signals trust, not surveillance."

**"You said the HR system is SAP — how does Anchor integrate?"**
> "AWS Lambda reads the new-starter trigger from SAP when someone is added. That auto-starts the Anchor onboarding flow — no manual steps, no HR admin overhead. We've scoped this integration specifically because SAP is TasNetworks' confirmed HR platform."

**"Is this feasible to actually implement?"**
> "The pilot is running by Week 1 — Typeform, Notion, Slack. Zero new technology. The AWS app is a 6-month build with a clear, fundable scope. We're asking for 90 days to prove the ROI with real data before a single dollar goes to infrastructure."

**"What stops buddies from not engaging?"**
> "The buddy role is opt-in and we recommend formalising it — 2 hours per fortnight of protected time, recognised in performance conversations. The matching quality means buddies actually want to engage — James's opening message in the demo is genuine because the match is real, not random."

**"How do you know the 26 early exits include relocated employees?"**
> "We don't have the exact breakdown — and we've called that out in our data request. But the exit survey shows 21% left for personal reasons unrelated to the job. That's the proxy for relocation hardship. Even if only half of those 26 are relocating employees, at $81,000 average replacement cost, that's $1M+ of annual risk. The pilot will give us the exact split within 90 days."

**"Why would someone score honestly in a pulse check if TasNetworks can see it?"**
> "The messaging is explicit: this goes to your People Partner, not your leader. And it's framed as care, not surveillance. The demo shows exactly that — 'I'm flagging this for your People Partner. Not to get you in trouble — the opposite.' The data shows 65% of leavers were comfortable with their exit survey data being shared with their leader. With the right framing, people do share. They just need to be asked."
